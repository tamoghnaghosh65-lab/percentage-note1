2. The Golden Percentage Trick

x% of y = y% of x
Example: 16% of 25 = 25% of 16 = 16 × 1/4 = 4.

3. Successive Percentage Change Trick

Two increases: Net change = a + b + (ab/100)%
Example: Increase by 20%, then 10%.
20 + 10 + (20 × 10)/100 = 32% increase.
Increase a%, then decrease b%: Net change = a − b − (ab/100)%
Example: +20%, then −10%.
20 − 10 − 2 = 8% increase.
Important: +20% followed by −20% is not zero.
20 − 20 − 4 = 4% decrease.

4. Reverse Percentage Trick

If a value increases by 25%, it becomes 125% of its original value. The decrease needed to return to the original is 25/125 × 100 = 20%.

5. Expenditure–Consumption Trick

Price rises by x% → consumption decrease = x/(100 + x) × 100
Example: Price rises by 25%.
25/125 × 100 = 20% consumption decrease to keep expenditure constant.
Reverse: Price falls by 20%.
Consumption can increase by 20/80 × 100 = 25%.

6. Base Value Trick

When the original value is unknown, assume it is 100. Convert the percentage change into a new value, then scale it.

Example: A number increases by 30% and becomes 260. Find the original.
Assume original = 100 → new value = 130.
130 corresponds to 260, so original = 260 × 100/130 = 200.

7. Population / Salary / Price Questions

For repeated growth, multiply the growth factors.

Final value = Initial value × (1 + r/100)n
Example: Population = 20,000; annual growth = 10%; period = 2 years.
20,000 × 1.1 × 1.1 = 20,000 × 1.21 = 24,200.

8. Marks Percentage Trick

Example: A student scores 35% and fails by 20 marks. Another scores 45% and gets 20 marks more than passing marks. Find total marks.
Score difference = 45% − 35% = 10%.
Marks difference = 20 + 20 = 40.
Thus 10% = 40 → 100% = 400 marks.
